## 2. Overall verdict

- **Authorship / resistance to generic “AI look”: 6.5/10, provisional.** Higher means more clearly authored. The product has a recognizable core, but the library is overloaded and supporting surfaces have less product-specific character. This is not a completed all-screen visual score.
- **Functionality risk: HIGH.** Audio recovery can invalidate already-submitted events without rebuilding the practice timeline; a progress-load failure can lead to destructive overwrite. Neither scenario was reproduced on hardware.
- **Release readiness: Not ready.** The icon set is empty, privacy declarations/policy access are missing, and key runtime release checks remain unverified. A successful unsigned Debug build does not resolve these.

### B. Functionality assessment

The normal-path structure is sound: host timestamps drive scheduled sound; display refresh is not the audio clock; `patternFrame` samples repeat identity and tape distance together (`PracticeSession.swift:243`); scoring probes copies before assigning a boundary hit once (`PracticeSession.swift:549`); completed passes alone receive credit, and section loops do not earn stars (`PracticeSession.swift:356`, `:502`). The previously reported tape hitch cannot be declared fixed or still present from source inspection alone. **Actual frame pacing and played-hit continuity are Not verified.**

Saved BPM and completed learning credit survive leaving a lesson (`PracticeSession.swift:597`, `Persistence.swift:858`). Listen/From memory, section-loop selection and metronome/pattern toggles are session-local and reset on reopening (`PracticeSession.swift:63`); this is stated behavior, not reported as a saved-progress bug. The star ledger preserves previous awards with boolean OR (`Persistence.swift:893`). Migration keeps earned flags and retires duplicate IDs (`Persistence.swift:753`); destructive recovery after a load failure is a separate problem below.

No reachable force-unwrap crash was established. For example, `best!.2` has a short-circuit nil guard, and the library BPM unwrap is guarded by a positive optional value. Those are not padded into crash findings. Hardware latency has no measured result here.

## 4. Top five changes for a more clearly authored app

1. **Repair composition before decorating it.** Make the Library header/filters and Practice toolbar respond to the actual iPad window and text size. A clipped custom design looks unfinished regardless of its palette or typeface. Keep all important filters available through a deliberate arrangement rather than shrinking their text.
2. **Let real rhythm connect Library to Practice.** A small preview for the selected/next lesson, using the existing hit notation and actual lesson data, would do more for identity than more progress rules or category icons. Do not turn every row into an illustrated card.
3. **Edit the typography rather than replace the brand.** Keep the broad Avenir headings and stable numeric rhythm; reduce the number of heavy headings and use monospace where its alignment actually helps. Narrative instructions need a more comfortable reading treatment and room for Dynamic Type.
4. **Give supporting screens the practice task's priorities.** Settings should answer “Can I hear and play my controller?”, Intro “What should I play first?”, and Calibration “Listen now or tap now?”. Use the same flat geometry, spacing and notation vocabulary without forcing every destination into an identical title/section stack.
5. **Make Full a concrete musical purchase.** Explain what is added, retain one visible local price and Restore, show the relevant curriculum/real rhythm, and describe stage progression plainly. Do not add testimonials, marketing adjectives, decorative benefit icons or a generic subscription-style paywall.

These are review recommendations only; no redesign has been implemented.

## 5. What is already good and must not change

- **The dominant orange rhythm surface with black/white hand encoding.** This is the clearest authored decision. Keep both light and dark surroundings; do not dilute it into a collection of neutral dashboard cards.
- **The controller-first model.** Large stable transport, continuous practice, on-demand utilities and a subordinate touch-pad diagram suit an external MIDI player. Do not promote the bonus touch pads into the main screen.
- **The real curriculum and specific lesson names.** “Kick on every beat” and “Hands take turns” are concrete. Keep open list rows, meaningful ordering and actual data rather than decorative category covers.
- **The selected typefaces and flat surfaces are deliberate.** Avenir Next Heavy plus Menlo is not untouched default SwiftUI typography. Refine hierarchy; do not replace it with the standard rounded-card/system-font kit. Native system controls are not inherently an AI-design defect.
- **Continuous clock-based tape and sound scheduling.** Preserve the separation between rendering and audible event times while fixing lifecycle recovery. Do not “smooth” timing with a UI animation that drifts from audio.
- **Progress and purchase principles.** Earned stars accumulate, learning credit is stored per pad count, migrated access is preserved, free access does not depend on product-price loading, purchase ownership is verified through StoreKit, and Restore is visible. Keep these invariants while improving recovery and proving the release flows.
- **Inline results and honest purchase copy.** Keep the player in the practice context, retain next-star guidance, a one-time payment, no subscription, clear price and a visible exit. A separate celebratory results dashboard or forced onboarding carousel would weaken the product.
